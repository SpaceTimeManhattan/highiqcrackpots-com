Satellite functions — written out

A probe (MESSENGER historically; BepiColombo next) approaches a planet that carries a radiation belt. Ordinary engineering practice treats the moments of belt puncture as corrupt and throws them away. SEXA Math / HEXAD does the opposite: those moments are the functions.

  1. Approach (outside the belt). All six HEXAD channels sit outside the belt envelope. They record a non-biased baseline — field, attitude, and residual — against which every later reading is gated.
  2. Threshold / pre-puncture. As the craft nears the belt edge, the synthetic frame spikes and briefly matches the radiation signature. That match is the tell: the stack is locking onto the envelope before entry.
  3. Puncture (one channel in, five out). One sensor channel crosses the belt wall. The other five stay outside that puncture. The crossing channel carries the biased transit; the five outsiders keep the unbiased reference. That is why six are required — a quaternion four-pack cannot hold both the punch and the outside stack at once.
  4. Inside the belt. Telemetry that used to be labeled trash is kept. Inside, the synthetic stack can approach a near-complete gravity reading. Admissibility gates decide what is allowed to count.
  5. Exit. On leaving the belt the radiation reading drops and the frame returns toward the synthetic outside state. Before / during / after must all be processed together; exit alone is not a solution.
  6. Close the loop. Compare after to before, with the during window as primary signal. The algebraic / synthetic calculus turns the three windows into a solvable number system for artificial gravity — not discarded noise.

Function summary: spike → brief radiation match → puncture with five outsiders → keep inside data → exit drop → solve across before / during / after.
